Landlord Deposit Deduction Kit

Keep only what you can defend. A wrong deduction doesn't just cost you the amount — in several states it costs you double or triple, plus the tenant's attorney fees. This kit turns a move-out into a defensible file: diagnose whether each deduction is lawful, generate the itemized statement, and validate it before anything goes in the mail.

A preparation tool, not legal advice. State rules change and local ordinances can be stricter — confirm your state's current statute before you send. Deadlines and penalties below are drawn from the statutes cited in each section.

The wear-vs-damage cheat sheet

Usually wear (don't deduct): light scuffs, faded paint, worn carpet in traffic lanes, dusty blinds, small nail holes, worn finish on floors from normal use, appliance age.

Usually damage (deductible with proof): large holes in walls, burns, broken fixtures, pet-stained carpet beyond cleaning, unauthorized paint colors, missing items, damage documented as new at move-in and broken at move-out.

The test a judge applies: did the tenant leave it worse than ordinary use would — and can you prove it with before/after evidence? If either answer is soft, cut the deduction. A smaller defensible statement beats a bigger one that gets thrown out whole.
